Here's the thing about the Claude vs ChatGPT debate that most articles get wrong: they treat it like a spec sheet comparison. More tokens here, better plugins there. As if you're buying a laptop.

But I've been using both tools for actual work for a while now — writing articles, editing drafts, doing research, summarising documents, the usual — and the difference isn't really about specs at all. It's about personality. And personality, it turns out, matters a lot when you're working with something all day.

Let me tell you what I actually noticed.

The thing about Claude that caught me off guard

I expected Claude to be the "safer" AI. The careful one. The one that hedges everything and refuses to commit to an opinion.

It's the opposite.

Claude will tell you when your idea is bad. Not in a brutal way — it's not rude — but it won't just validate everything you say like an eager intern trying to keep their job. I sent it a blog post I was quite pleased with and it came back with: "The structure here is solid but the opening buries the most interesting part. You might want to lead with paragraph four." It was right. That's the kind of feedback I'd pay a good editor for.

ChatGPT, in my experience, has a tendency to tell you things are good when they're not. It's agreeable. Sometimes painfully so. Ask it to review something and it'll say "This is excellent! Here are a few minor suggestions..." even when the thing is genuinely mediocre. It wants you to like it. Which is sweet, and also useless.

"Claude will tell you your idea is bad. ChatGPT will help you execute a bad idea very efficiently."

Where ChatGPT is genuinely better

Okay, so I'm not here to just sell you on Claude. ChatGPT does things Claude can't — and some of them matter quite a bit.

The image generation is real. DALL-E is built right into ChatGPT, which means you can go from "write me ad copy for this product" to "now generate a visual to go with it" in the same conversation. That's not a small thing if you're doing content work.

The plugin ecosystem is also massive. You can connect ChatGPT to your calendar, your documents, external databases — it plays well with other tools in a way Claude currently doesn't. If your workflow involves a lot of different apps talking to each other, ChatGPT is ahead.

And coding. If you write code — or want AI to write code for you — ChatGPT with its code interpreter is still ahead. Not by miles, but enough to matter if that's your primary use case.

// The thing I keep coming back to

Most comparison articles act like you have to choose one. But the two tools genuinely complement each other. I use Claude to think and write. I use ChatGPT to build and make things. That's the honest split.

So — which one?

If you write things for a living — blog posts, emails, marketing copy, reports — use Claude. The quality difference in long-form writing is real, and the fact that it'll actually push back on you makes the end product better.
